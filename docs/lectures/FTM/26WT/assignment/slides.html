Task

You need to introduce an emerging digital technology so that your audience gains a solid understanding of it.

Each student is assigned a technology, see Table 1. There is no topic proposal in this term.

In your presentation of approx. 8 minutes, cover

  • what the technology is and how it works,
  • its key characteristics (i.e., based on the characteristics discussed in the lecture notes),
  • and its current state of development and maturity.

You can include grey literature1, but you need to analyze at least three (3) scientific papers.

The Q&A

After your presentation, I will ask you questions for approx. 8 minutes.

The questions check how well you have understood the lecture contents. They address the emergent nature of the technology you presented and its relation to the hypotheses discussed in the lecture notes.

You are expected to answer in a theory-based way: use the terminology and the concepts from the lecture notes, and argue from them rather than from intuition or from general technology news.

  • Classify your technology against the five attributes of emerging technologies
  • Argue which of the hypotheses your technology supports, and where that support is weak
  • Relate your technology to a hypothesis it does not obviously fit
  • Derive fields of application and social or ethical implications from the concepts discussed

All four hypotheses are examinable, not only the one your technology fits most obviously.

Example question: You argue that your technology enables immersive experiences. However, considering Milgram & Kishino’s virtuality continuum and the role of tactile stimuli discussed in the lecture notes, where exactly would you place it, and what sensory limitations prevent deeper immersion?

Preparing only your own technology is not sufficient. The questions move across the four hypotheses and back to the attributes of emergence, so work through all five units of the summer term 2026.

The presentation of the technology (approx. 8 min., 30%)
+ the Q&A (approx. 8 min., 70%)

  • your presentation of the assigned technology (approx. 8 minutes, 30% of your grade)

The weighting reflects what is examined. The presentation establishes a shared understanding of the technology; the Q&A establishes whether you can reason about it with the theory from the lecture.

Scientific literature

In your presentation, you need to consider at least three scientific papers.

  • Select only papers that are peer-reviewed.
  • Search in the field that actually studies your technology, see Table 2. Most of the assigned technologies are researched in engineering, computer science, physics or materials science, so the information systems journals will return little on the technology itself.
  • In computer science and human-computer interaction, peer-reviewed conference papers rank alongside or above journals. CHI, UIST, SenSys and NeurIPS are full scientific sources, not preliminary ones.
  • Start with a review or survey article, which gives you the state of the art in a single source. ACM Computing Surveys, Proceedings of the IEEE and the Annual Review series are good entry points.
  • Use Web of Science, Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore and the ACM Digital Library (through the HNU library proxy) to search for literature.
  • Use the citation count as an indicator of significance2, but correct for age: a 2025 paper cannot have accumulated the citations of a 2015 one, and your technologies are recent.
