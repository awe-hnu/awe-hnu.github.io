Task

During the semester you will be required to work on a business challenge posed by a company partner. As a group, you

  • gather information about the practical context of the business challenge,
  • research information about the topic (state of research and practice),
  • identify and understand stakeholders that are affected by the problem as well as their needs,
  • develop an innovative, agent-based approach to tackle the problem,
  • detail the solution and create a running prototype,
  • create an implementation plan,
  • detail and reflect on the specific value creation mechanisms (e.g., monetarization opportunities, cost savings, etc.), and
  • discuss lessons learnt, contributions and practical recommendations for action.

Deliverables

As a group, you will deliver a final presentation and a seminar paper.

  • The presentation gets to the heart of your idea (25% of the grade):
    • Short summary of your concept (i.e., elevator pitch)
    • Presentation of the prototype of your design
  • The seminar paper is a detailed documentation of your approach and of the artefact (75%):

The grade is the weighted average of the group score (60%) and the individual contributions (40%); the individual contributions must be indicated.

Seminar paper

Contents and structure

  • Abstract: Gives a short elevator pitch of the approach that sums up your concept in a nutshell.

  • Introduction: Provides an introduction that piques the reader’s interest or curiosity for what is to come by indicating the main challenge.

  • Problem definition: Outlines the specific problems that you have identified and that your solution is intended to address, as well as the stakeholders that will be affected and their needs. Also be clear about the consequences of failing to solve the challenge. You synthesise these into a concise problem statement. There is a need for reference to (scientific) literature here.

  • Approach: Describe and discuss your approach to using IT to address the challenge and to create value for the organisation. You also need to consider alternatives to what you propose. Show why your approach is different from the alternatives and why it is more promising.

  • Solution: Provides a detailed description of the solution, including technical details. In this section you should clearly show how the (characteristics of the) solution will support or enable your approach to IT-enabled value creation. Please also be specific in your description of the innovative nature of your solution.

  • Implementation: This section covers how the organisation should and has implemented your solution, including a discussion of any potential hurdles that may need to be overcome. Also include how you plan to evaluate effectiveness.

  • Discussion: Presents lessons learned, contributions and practical recommendations for action that go beyond the specific challenge. This involves looking at (scientific) literature.

  • Appendix: Provides information that may help to gain an even better understanding of your solution.

  • Abstract
  • Introduction and problem definition (challenge, needs, status quo)
  • Approach (value creation mechanism)
  • Solution (detailed description)
  • Implementation (value realization)
  • Discussion
  • Appendix

Evaluation criteria

An exemplary seminar paper has the following characteristics

  • The intro makes the reader want to read further
  • The problem definition is concise and comprehensive
  • The reader is able to picture the stakeholder and their needs
  • The approach to value creation is fully elaborated, complete and makes sense
  • The characteristics of the solution relate well to the problem and the approach
  • The solution has the maturity level of an MVP (value creation can be demonstrated)
  • The description of the solution allow for a detailed understanding
  • The recommendations for value realization as well as their rationale make sense
  • The solution and the process are well reflected (lessons learned and contributions)
  • There are very few errors, the writing is clear, and easy to read and engaging
  • The originality of the solution is clear and the used sources are evident

Business challenge

General Briefing

Alongside teaching and research, transfer is the third strategic core task of Hochschule Neu-Ulm (HNU). Transfer is understood as the continuous exchange of ideas, knowledge and technologies between the university and actors from business, society, research and politics.

The Referat Projektentwicklung, Transfer und Kooperationen (Office for Project Development, Transfer and Cooperation) supports this exchange by bringing together enquiries, contacts, cooperations and activities from inside and outside the university. Its aim is to connect people, knowledge and ideas, and to turn these connections into value for teaching, research and practice.

The Challenge

Every day, HNU’s transfer activities generate new contacts, cooperations, project ideas and insights. At the same time, the sheer number of actors, topics and processes is becoming an organisational challenge.

Enquiries from inside and outside the university reach the office through many channels: e-mail, telephone, personal contact. They concern cooperation opportunities, research and practice projects, knowledge transfer, third-party funded projects, events or networking activities. Matching an enquiry to the right counterpart largely depends on personal experience and individual networks.

  • Information on contacts, competencies, interests, existing cooperations and ongoing activities is scattered across people, departments and systems, and is therefore only partly visible
  • Suitable people, departments or partners may be overlooked, and valuable synergies remain unused
  • Knowledge that is not followed up immediately is easily forgotten, although other researchers, lecturers, students or units might benefit from it

This is particularly relevant when third-party funded projects are initiated. Most funding programmes require suitable partners from business, research or society. The search for them currently builds on existing contacts and personal experience, as a consolidated overview of networks, competencies, earlier cooperations and potential project partners exists only to a limited extent.

The transfer area also hosts the Career Service. Companies and organisations regularly want to offer job vacancies, internships, thesis topics, guest lectures or excursions to students. These offers are currently distributed and maintained across several channels. For companies it is not always evident how to place their offer best, while the university carries a high communication and administration effort.

In addition, numerous transfer events are organised by professors, faculties, competence centres and central units. They are important touchpoints between university, business and society and continuously produce new ideas, contacts, questions and cooperation opportunities. So far, the insights gained are captured and processed only in a limited and unstructured way. Standardised means are missing to

  • record feedback and needs of participants,
  • make new topics and challenges visible,
  • track follow-up activities and cooperations,
  • make insights available university-wide, and
  • assess the long-term impact of individual events.

Valuable hints on current challenges in companies and society, new research ideas or cooperation potential arise especially in personal conversations before, during or after an event, and usually stay with the person who had the conversation.

This raises the question of how knowledge gained in transfer can flow back into the university. Insights from events, conversations, cooperation projects or third-party funded projects could provide important impulses for new transfer formats, research activities, project ideas or course offerings. Currently there is no systematic way of making these connections visible and of tracing how knowledge from business and society actually influences teaching, research and future transfer activities.

HNU already operates a CRM system (Adito) for managing contact and partner data. The diverse requirements of the transfer area can be mapped in it only to a limited extent, while relevant contacts must still be maintained centrally and in compliance with data protection law. Capturing new contacts and updating existing records currently requires considerable manual effort, which undermines sustainable data quality and reduces the timeliness and usability of the available information.

Finally, there is the wish to evaluate transfer activities and their impact systematically. Among others, the following questions arise:

  • From which areas do enquiries and cooperations originate?
  • Which topics and fields of competence are in particularly high demand?
  • Which target groups are reached?
  • Which contacts, projects or cooperations emerge from them?
  • Which partners are suitable for future third-party funded and research projects?
  • Which topics are developing into future fields of demand?
  • What effects do transfer activities have on research, teaching and further transfer formats?
  • Which activities create the greatest value for HNU and its partners?

Considering these challenges, the Referat Projektentwicklung, Transfer und Kooperationen raises the following question:

How can agentic AI be leveraged to capture, connect and evaluate enquiries, contacts, knowledge, events and cooperation opportunities, so that relevant knowledge is not lost, suitable actors are brought together, new cooperations emerge, and the impact of transfer activities becomes visible?

Mandatory Questions

The following questions should definitely be answered as part of your concept and addressed by your MVP:

  • Who are the relevant stakeholders and what are their (unmet) needs?
  • How can enquiries, contacts, knowledge and cooperation opportunities be captured and connected transparently?
  • What data is available and can be used, and which data has to be created in the first place?
  • What capabilities do AI agents bring in and how can these effectively used?
  • How can knowledge from transfer activities be made usable for future events, cooperations, research and teaching?
  • How can contact and partner data be managed efficiently while sensibly taking existing systems (in particular the CRM in use) into account?
  • How can we ensure the solution complies with relevant data protection regulations while handling personal contact and conversation data?
  • Which open-source components are suitable for an MIT/Apache-2.0-licensed system?
  • What is the potential impact (i.e., business value) of your solution for HNU and its stakeholders, and how can this value be measured?
  • How does the MVP proof your concept, particularly regarding technical feasability and value add?
  • What factors must be taken into consideration to ensure the successful implementation and ongoing maintenance of the solution, especially regarding data quality and adoption?

Requirements

Networking of actors
The solution should connect actors, contacts and enquiries efficiently and support the identification of suitable counterparts and cooperation partners.
Knowledge retention
Knowledge and insights from transfer activities (events, conversations, projects) must be secured sustainably, i.e., they remain findable and reusable beyond the person who gathered them.
Low-effort data maintenance
The solution should simplify the maintenance of contact and partner data and reduce manual effort; existing systems must be taken into account rather than replaced light-heartedly.
Measure of impact
It is essential to introduce metrics that allow to evaluate the effectiveness and performance of the solution as well as to create transparency about transfer activities and their effects.
Security and data privacy
The solution processes personal data of internal and external stakeholders and must implement appropriate security measures and comply with the GDPR.
Technology foundation
The solution should utilize open-source technologies for core AI/ML components and follow modern software architecture principles (API-first, microservices).

Goals and Outcome

The goal of this challenge is to develop new approaches for HNU’s transfer management: solutions that effectively connect people, knowledge, ideas and cooperation opportunities. A successful solution will:

  • Make knowledge from transfer activities usable in the long term
  • Improve the exchange between internal and external stakeholders
  • Foster cooperations and third-party funded activities
  • Strengthen the data basis for strategic decisions
  • Support the flow of insights back into teaching, research and future transfer formats

Common Transfer Enquiries

To better understand the types of requests the solution needs to address, here are common examples from the transfer office:

Cooperation & Networking

  • We are looking for a university partner for a joint research or development project.
  • Who at HNU works on artificial intelligence / sustainability / cyber security?
  • Are there researchers or students with expertise in our field of interest?
  • Can you support us in finding a project partner?

Third-Party Funding & Research

  • We would like to submit a funding proposal and are looking for suitable practice or research partners.
  • Which competencies exist at HNU in the field of X?
  • Are there previous projects or experiences on this topic?
  • Who could become part of a project consortium?

Career Service

  • We would like to advertise an internship or a working student position.
  • How can we attract students for our thesis topic?
  • We would like to offer a guest lecture or an excursion.
  • Which options are there to make our company visible to students?

Events & Knowledge Transfer

  • We are looking for speakers for an event on a specific topic.
  • Which topics are currently of particular interest to companies?
  • Is there demand for a new transfer or event format?
  • Which contacts or follow-up activities emerged from a past event?

General Transfer Enquiries

  • We have a challenge in our company and are looking for scientific support.
  • Is there expertise at HNU on our topic?
  • Who could be the right contact person for our enquiry?
  • Which options for collaboration with HNU exist?
