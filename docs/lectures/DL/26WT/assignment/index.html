Task

You are assigned a leader. You select one specific leadership situation involving that leader and present it.

Your task in the presentation is to portray the situation accurately and completely enough that it can be analyzed. The analysis itself happens afterwards, in the Q&A.

The presentation must take approximately 8 minutes. It is followed by an approximately 8-minute Q&A with the examiner, in which you analyze the situation you presented using the theories discussed in the course.

The Core Idea

Complex situations require multiple mental models (latticework). The assignment places weight on understanding and transfer: you have to be able to explain the theories without your slides, apply them to the situation you brought, and justify every analytical claim with evidence from it.

Because the analysis happens in the Q&A, a polished description of a situation you cannot analyze will not carry you. Choose a situation you understand deeply.

Leaders

The allocation is also announced via mail (see schedule).

Situation

You select the situation yourself. A situation suitable for analysis has

  • a clear decision or turning point, not a general account of a career,
  • identifiable actors whose interests and positions can be reconstructed,
  • public documentation you can cite: reporting, interviews, memos, filings, testimony,
  • enough detail to support more than one reading, so that different theories reveal different things.

Avoid episodes that are purely biographical, that are so recent they are still thinly documented, or that are so well known that you cannot add anything beyond the summary everyone already has.

Narrow is better than broad. A single week in which a decision was made and reversed gives you more to analyze than a five-year tenure.

Presentation

In approximately 8 minutes, convey

  • who the leader is in the relevant period: role, formal authority, and situation they inherited,
  • what happened: the sequence of events, the decision or turning point, and what followed,
  • who was involved and what was at stake for them,
  • what is contested: where sources disagree, or where motives are disputed.

Do not present a theoretical analysis on your slides. Describe the situation; the theory comes in the Q&A.

You can include grey literature, but you need to ground the situation in sources you can cite and defend.

Q&A

Structure

The presentation is followed by an approximately 8-minute Q&A. I ask you to analyze the situation you just presented using the theories discussed in the course.

You cannot pre-select which theory you will be asked for. I choose the prompts after hearing your presentation, based on what the situation actually offers.

Expect to be asked to explain a theory in its own terms, to apply it to your situation, to say what it reveals and what it misses, and to reason about what would have been different had a condition not held.

Topics

Any theory from Units 1 to 8 may be invoked. The eight dimensions below are the territory:

  • Self-awareness and leader traits
  • Behavioral complexity and competing values
  • Engaging leadership and motivation
  • Team building and psychological safety
  • Political navigation, power, and conflict
  • Stakeholder management and social capital
  • Cultural intelligence and context
  • Storytelling and communication

Not all eight apply to any one situation. Part of what is assessed is whether you can say which apply to yours and why the others do not.

Preparation

  • Know your situation in depth: the sequence, the actors, the evidence, and the points where sources disagree.
  • Revisit all course units well enough to apply any of their theories to your situation on short notice.
  • For each theory that plausibly fits, work out in advance what it reveals about your situation and where it reaches its limits.
  • Think through counterfactuals: what would the theory predict if a key fact had been different?
  • Be able to explain every theory you invoke without your slides, including its boundary conditions.

A situation you chose yourself gives you no advantage if you have only read about it. The Q&A rewards having thought about it.

Submission

  • Submit your presentation slides (.pptx) via Email until the deadline.
  • Use the following naming scheme: DL_WT26_Re-Exam_Surname-Name
  • You will present the slides you submitted (no late updates).
  • For the deadline and the presentation date, please see the schedule.

Grading

Presentation (30%)

An excellent presentation has the following characteristics:

  • The situation is portrayed concisely and accurately, with the sequence of events clear.
  • The leader’s role, formal authority, and inherited circumstances are established.
  • The actors involved and what was at stake for each of them are identified.
  • Contested points are named, and the sources behind them are cited and defensible.
  • The situation chosen offers genuine analytical purchase rather than a familiar summary.
  • The presenter communicates clearly, uses visual aids effectively, and stays within time.

Q&A (70%)

An excellent performance in the Q&A has the following characteristics:

  • Theories are explained precisely and confidently without slides, including their boundary conditions.
  • Theories are applied to explain why the leader acted as they did and what followed, rather than relabeling events in theoretical vocabulary.
  • Each analytical claim is traced back to specific evidence from the presented situation.
  • What a theory reveals and where it reaches its limits are both articulated.
  • Changed conditions are analyzed consistently with the theories, leading to plausible, reasoned predictions.
  • Theories that do not fit the situation are recognised as such, with reasons.

Minimum Requirement

If you cannot apply any course theory to the situation you presented yourself, the assessment is graded as failed (5.0), irrespective of the quality of the slides.

Formal Requirements

Compliance with formal requirements and good scientific practice are critical pass criteria, meaning that in case of non-compliance the exam is automatically failed (e.g., plagiarism, even light forms).

A Note on Grades

Grade Meaning
1: very good A truly outstanding achievement that (not only) shows no deficiencies in the criteria mentioned, but also gives both the supervisor and external assessors an excellent impression.
2: good Work that exceeds the average requirements/performance and is easily recognizable and presentable to the outside world as a “good performance”.
Note 2.5 is the average of passed assessments, i.e., an “average performance”
3: satisfactory A performance that achieves the desired goal “to a satisfactory extent”; however, deficiencies can be identified here and there.
4: sufficient A performance that “still adequately satisfies” the requirements, but deviates from the expectations placed on it in several ways.
5: not sufficient A performance that does not meet several of the criteria mentioned.
